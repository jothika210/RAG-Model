# HR-205 — Parental Leave Addendum (AMER)

Region: AMER
Effective Date: 2026-01-01
Policy ID: HR-205

## 1. Purpose

This addendum supplements the base Employee Leave Handbook for employees working in the AMER region (United States, Canada, Mexico, Brazil). It sets out parental leave entitlement for birth, adoption, and foster placement.

### 1.1 Scope

Applies to all full-time employees who have completed at least 6 months of continuous service at the start of leave. Part-time employees are covered under Section 3.3.

## 2. Entitlement

### 2.1 Primary Caregiver

Primary caregivers are entitled to 16 weeks of paid parental leave at full salary, inclusive of any applicable statutory leave (e.g. FMLA in the United States runs concurrently, not additively).

### 2.2 Secondary Caregiver

Secondary caregivers are entitled to 6 weeks of paid parental leave at full salary.

## 3. Eligibility Table

| Employee Category | Minimum Service | Leave Type | Weeks Paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-time, primary caregiver | 6 months | Birth/Adoption/Foster | 16 |
| Full-time, secondary caregiver | 6 months | Birth/Adoption/Foster | 6 |
| Part-time (>=20 hrs/week), primary | 6 months | Birth/Adoption/Foster | 8 (pro-rata) |
| Contractor / agency staff | Not eligible | — | 0 |

### 3.1 Notes

"Primary caregiver" is the parent who will be the child's main provider of care during the leave period, regardless of gender. Employees must designate primary or secondary status in writing to People Ops at least 30 days before the expected leave start date where practicable.

### 3.2 Adoption and Foster Placement

Leave entitlement under Section 2 applies equally to adoption and approved foster placements of children under 18, calculated from the date of legal placement rather than birth date.

### 3.3 Part-Time Employees

Part-time employees working 20 or more hours per week are eligible per the table in Section 3, with weeks paid pro-rated to contracted hours. Part-time employees under 20 hours per week are not eligible for company-paid parental leave but may access unpaid statutory leave where locally mandated.

## 4. Carry-Over

Parental leave is a per-event entitlement, not an annual accrual, and therefore has no carry-over provision.
